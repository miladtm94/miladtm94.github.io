We study a full-duplex ISAC network facing a proactive, trajectory-planning UAV eavesdropper (M-UAV) that wiretaps both downlink and uplink users while avoiding obstacles and saving flight energy. The interaction is modeled as a non-cooperative Stackelberg game in which the M-UAV leads and the ISAC base station follows, allocating beamforming, artificial noise, and power subject to secrecy-rate and CRLB sensing constraints. The follower problem is solved with a low-complexity successive convex approximation (SDR proven rank-one tight), while the leader's trajectory is learned with double deep Q-learning. Results show that the artificial-noise-assisted design cuts network power by about 13%, is robust to clutter, and neutralizes the adaptive adversary compared with a non-adaptive baseline.
